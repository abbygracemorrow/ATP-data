About the data

Where the data comes from and how every number is computed

Source and what one row is

The data is the ATP Tennis 2000–2023 Daily Pull by dissfya on Kaggle — a dataset the uploader refreshes daily, so this copy runs past the “2023” in its name: {{rows_total}} ATP men's tour matches from {{first_year}} through {{last_date}}, with {{players}} players and {{tournaments}} tournaments. I kept 13 of its columns and changed no existing values; the one exception is a single hand-added row, sourced and explained below.

One row is one main-draw match, with the tournament, date, series, court, surface, round, best-of format, the two players, the winner, both players' ATP rankings, and the score. The loser is the other player. The order of Player_1 and Player_2 carries no information (Player_1 wins {{p1_wins}} of matches), so no statistic depends on it, and the score is not used.

Rows dropped and rows missing

  • No rows were dropped. The ranking finding leaves out {{rk_excluded}} matches where a ranking is not listed or both players have the same ranking.
  • A full Slam draw has 127 matches. The file still has {{slam_missing}} fewer than the {{slam_expected}} expected ({{slam_missing_pct}}), almost all of them early rounds; those are left “as recorded,” not adjusted.
  • One match was missing outright, not just uncounted: the 2019 US Open final. I added that single row by hand — Nadal d. Medvedev, 7–5, 6–3, 5–7, 4–6, 6–4, from the official ATP Tour match record — so Nadal's {{t1_total}} titles above include it. It is the only row in the file that didn't come from the Kaggle download; every other number in this report still comes only from that download.
  • Wimbledon was cancelled in 2020, and the {{last_year}} US Open had not started when this copy of the Kaggle dataset was pulled, on {{last_date}}.

Definitions

  • Grand Slam match: a row where Series is “Grand Slam” (Australian Open, French Open or Roland Garros, Wimbledon, and US Open). Title: a win in a Grand Slam row where Round is “The Final”.
  • Career Grand Slam: at least one title at each of the four Slams.
  • Win rate: wins ÷ matches played. Conversion rate: titles ÷ finals played.
  • Percentage points: the difference between two percentages. Median: the middle value when a list is sorted.
  • Higher-ranked player: the player with the smaller ranking number. Ranking gap: the difference between the two ranking numbers. Average ranking: the sum of the listed rankings ÷ how many are listed.

Thresholds and notes

  • Best-of-five rates need at least {{min_bo5}} matches ({{n_q5}} players qualify). Best-of-three rates need at least {{min_bo3}} ({{n_q3}} qualify). The gap chart needs {{min_bo5}} best-of-five and {{min_bo3_gap}} best-of-three matches ({{gap_n}} qualify).
  • Streak: Grand Slam matches won in a row, in date order and across tournaments. A loss resets it, and round order breaks ties on the same date.
  • Surface split: counted only for Grand Slam matches (Series = “Grand Slam”); every value in the Surface column at that tier is Hard, Clay, or Grass, mapping one-to-one to the Australian Open/US Open, French Open, and Wimbledon respectively — verified when the report is built, not assumed. The file has no player nationality or location data of any kind, which is why this report and the dashboard show no geographic information.
  • Dashboard tiers: International = ATP 250, International Gold = ATP 500, Masters = Masters 1000, and Masters Cup = Tour Finals.
